Finalizing Eviction: Obtaining a Writ of Possession in Pittsburg

Following a successful court result granting eviction, the next step is obtaining a Writ of Possession. In Pittsburg and throughout Contra Costa County, the local sheriff’s department is responsible for serving and enforcing the writ at the tenant’s rental property. The writ provides a specific deadline—generally five days—for the tenant to vacate. California law strictly prohibits landlords from any form of self-help; only the sheriff may carry out the physical eviction. For tenants, this notice period is the final opportunity to vacate without incurring further liability or facing forced removal. Landlords should coordinate with the sheriff’s office and consult their eviction attorney in Pittsburg to ensure all post-judgment procedures are followed, preventing accidental legal violations or additional liability.

How Long Does the Eviction Process Take in Pittsburg?

Eviction timelines in Pittsburg, CA, vary according to several factors: property location, notice requirements, and court scheduling in Contra Costa County. Properties in special zones—such as Old Town Pittsburg, near the BART station, or in large multi-family complexes—may be subject to additional municipal permitting or tenant protection regulations, which can extend or affect proceeding speed. The court’s docket load and the thoroughness of the case paperwork are also critical factors. Our eviction attorneys in Pittsburg proactively clarify these timelines and help clients plan realistic expectations, reducing stressful surprises or unnecessary waiting.

There are variables in every case. Whether or not the tenant contests the eviction notice plays a large role in how long everything ultimately takes. The schedule of the courts themselves is another factor. If a judge is backlogged, it can take some time for a case to be heard. And the length of time a tenant has to respond to the first written notice will also impact the ultimate timetable. 

Some general guidelines, though, are that once the case is filed in court and the tenant served with papers, an uncontested case will take 1-2 months, while a contested eviction can last 2-3 months.

Commonly Asked Questions

What is the minimum notice period a landlord must provide for eviction in California?

In California, the minimum notice period a landlord must provide to a tenant for eviction depends on the reason for the eviction. For unpaid rent or other lease violations, this period typically ranges from 3 to 60 days. The specific terms of the lease may also define the notice required. Both landlords and tenants in Pittsburg, CA, should always review their agreements and consult with an eviction attorney to ensure adherence to all legal requirements.

Can a tenant be served an eviction notice by having it pushed under their door in California?

Yes, California law allows service of an eviction notice by sliding it under the door—but only after a genuine attempt has been made to personally deliver the notice. This is a common issue in Pittsburg and throughout Contra Costa County. Proper service is essential, as mistakes at this stage can cause the entire case to be dismissed. If you are uncertain about serving or receiving notice, consult an eviction attorney in Pittsburg for clarity and to avoid legal setbacks.
