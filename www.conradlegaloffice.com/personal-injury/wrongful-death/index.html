Frequently Asked Questions

How Does California Define Wrongful Death?

In California, wrongful death is defined as a death caused by the negligence or misconduct of another person or entity. The law permits certain individuals, like spouses, children, or others who depend financially on the deceased, to file a claim. This legal action seeks compensation for their loss, including lost wages, loss of companionship, and funeral expenses. The aim is to hold the responsible party accountable and to provide financial support to the bereaved family.

What Is the Process of a Wrongful Death Lawsuit?

The process begins with a consultation with a fatal accident attorney in Pittsburg to determine the viability of the case. After identifying liable parties, a lawsuit is filed. The discovery phase follows, where both parties exchange information and evidence. Negotiations with insurance companies may occur, aiming to settle the case. If a fair settlement isn’t reached, the case proceeds to trial, where a judge or jury decides the outcome.
